Rabbits are born with five numbers. They get hungry, thirsty, and want to breed. Whatever keeps them alive long enough to reproduce gets passed on — with a small chance of mutation each generation. You set the conditions. The numbers decide who survives.

About this artifact

A tribute, not an original idea.

This whole thing exists because of one video: Sebastian Lague's Coding Adventure: Simulating an Ecosystem, published in 2019. Watching a population of cube rabbits stumble through hunger, thirst, and reproduction — and watching their genes actually drift in response to what killed them and what didn't — is what made evolution by natural selection feel less like a textbook diagram and more like something you could watch happen.

What stayed with me wasn't the polish, it was the mechanism: a handful of numbers with no instructions attached, filtered generation after generation by nothing more than who managed to eat, drink, and breed before their timer ran out. Change one setting — how far a rabbit can see, how long a plant takes to regrow, how fast a fox runs — and the entire population's genetic future bends around it. Small change, different species, a few generations later.

This build is my attempt to sit inside that idea a while longer: rebuilt in the browser with three.js, dressed with Polyfork's low-poly kit, and turned into something you can tune while it runs rather than only watch. None of the core concept is mine. The rabbits, the foxes, the hunger and thirst and gestation trade-offs, the whole shape of the simulation — that's Lague's, watched closely and rebuilt as a way of understanding it properly. If you haven't seen the original, it's fifteen minutes and it's worth every one of them.

Built with three.js · environment assets from Polyfork · not affiliated with Sebastian Lague
